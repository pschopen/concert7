Free reverb for classical music

Concert halls for your recordings.

Concert7 is a free reverb plugin for orchestra, choir, piano, chamber music and solo voice. It places your recordings in real recorded halls, chambers and rooms – and lets you shape each space: longer or shorter, warmer or brighter, closer or further away.

Made for the free Bricasti M7 impulse responses recorded by Samplicity, which you download separately.

What you can do with it

From the chamber to the church

Classical recordings are often made close up or in dry rooms. Concert7 gives them back the space they were meant for.

Orchestra in a hall

Give a dry or close-miked orchestra the depth and blend of a concert hall, with natural early reflections and a smooth tail.

Choir and organ

Long, spacious tails for sacred music – stretch a large hall well beyond its recorded length for a church-like acoustic.

Piano and solo instruments

A warm room or small hall that adds bloom without blurring fast passages. Shorten the bass decay to keep the left hand clear.

Chamber music and voice

Chambers and smaller halls for string quartets, Lieder and early music. A little pre-delay keeps attacks and diction clear.

Match your spot microphones

Give close microphones the same room as your main pair. Early reflection level and roll-off set how far away they sound.

Shape it like a real room

Let the highs die away faster than the lows, tame a boomy low end and fine-tune the colour with the built-in EQ.

Why Concert7

Recorded spaces, without the usual limits

Most reverbs either calculate a space or play back a recording of one.

A recording – an impulse response – captures every detail of a real space or a real hardware unit. But it is fixed: a two-second hall stays a two-second hall, and a bright plate stays bright.

Concert7 rebuilds the recording whenever you turn a knob. You can stretch or shorten it, darken its tail, move the reflections or borrow them from another space – and it still sounds like the space you started with.

It also keeps the full true stereo image of the recordings: a sound on the left side also excites the right side of the room, just like in reality.

Get started

Up and running in three steps

Concert7 itself is small. The sounds come from Samplicity's free impulse response library, which Concert7 installs for you.

Download Concert7

Choose your system and run the installer.

macOS Windows Linux

Get the impulse responses

Download the free Bricasti M7 library (one ZIP file, about 1 GB) from Samplicity. You don't need to unpack it. If you like it, please support Samplicity with a donation.

Go to Samplicity

Drop it in

Open Concert7 in your music software. A setup screen appears – drag the ZIP file onto it. Concert7 checks the file and installs the sounds. That's it.

macOS

10.13 or newer, Apple Silicon and Intel. AU, VST3 and standalone app.

Windows

Windows 10 or 11, 64 bit. VST3 and standalone app.

Linux

x86_64, e.g. Ubuntu 22.04 or newer. VST3, LV2 and standalone app.

Questions

Good to know

Is Concert7 really free?

Yes. Concert7 is open source software under the GNU GPL – no account, no time limit, no paid version. The impulse responses are offered free of charge by Samplicity; please see their page for their terms of use.

Why do I need to download the sounds separately?

The impulse responses are Samplicity's work and are distributed by them, not by Concert7. You download them once from Samplicity's page and drop the ZIP file onto Concert7 – it does the rest.

Which music software does it work with?

Any program that loads AU, VST3 or LV2 effect plugins. You can also use the standalone app without any other software. So far Concert7 has been tested by hand in REAPER on macOS – if something doesn't work in your setup, please let us know.

My computer warns me about the installer. Is that normal?

Yes – the installers are not signed with a paid developer certificate. On macOS, open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. On Windows, click More info → Run anyway.

Can I use my own impulse responses?

Yes. Put mono, stereo or four-channel files into Concert7's library folder and choose Rescan library in the setup menu – they appear in an extra User bank.

Can I save my own presets?

Not yet inside Concert7. All settings are saved with your project, and you can use your music software's preset function to keep favourites.

Does it work in surround?

No, Concert7 is a stereo plugin.

Is Concert7 made by Bricasti or Samplicity?

No. Concert7 is an independent project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Bricasti or Samplicity. It is simply made to play the impulse responses that Samplicity recorded and gives away for free. Bricasti and M7 are trademarks of their respective owner.

What does “beta” mean?

Version 0.1 is the first public release. It is ready to use, but you may find rough edges – reports in the issue tracker help a lot.

Want to know what every knob does?

The documentation explains each control in plain words, with ranges and tips.
